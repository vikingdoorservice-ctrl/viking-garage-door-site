Diagnose It Yourself

What's Wrong With Your Garage Door?

Find the symptom below. In almost every case you will know within thirty seconds what broke and roughly what it costs to fix.

You heard a loud bang from the garage

That gunshot sound is a torsion spring letting go — the single most common garage door failure in Spartanburg. Look at the bar above the door: you will see a two-inch gap in the coil. The door is now dead weight. Do not run the opener.

Fix: spring replacement, from $290.

Spring replacement details →

The opener clicks or hums but the door won't move

Usually a broken spring (see above) or a stripped drive gear inside the opener head. On chain-drive LiftMaster and Chamberlain units the nylon gear is the part that wears out first, typically after ten to fifteen years.

Fix: spring from $290, or opener repair from $120.

The door is crooked or came off the track

One side dropped, the door is wedged at an angle, and the rollers popped out. Caused by a snapped cable, a bump from a vehicle, or a loose lift-cable drum. Leave it alone — a door under tension in that position is genuinely dangerous.

Fix: from $150, most jobs 1–2 hours.

The door closes, touches down, then goes right back up

Ninety percent of the time it is the photo-eye sensors near the floor — knocked out of alignment by a bike or a trash can, or covered in dust and spider webs. The rest of the time it is the opener's down-force setting or the door binding on the track.

Fix: sensor alignment from $95.

Grinding, screeching, banging every time it moves

Steel rollers with worn bearings, dry hinges, or loose track bolts. Cheap fix now — an expensive one later, because a door running rough tears through cables and springs years early.

Fix: nylon roller set from $150, or a $120 tune-up.

Tune-up & maintenance →

The remote or keypad stopped working

Start with a fresh battery and reprogramming. If that fails it is the logic board, the antenna wire, or an outdated receiver. On openers older than 1997 there is no rolling-code security at all, which is worth knowing.

Fix: from $120, or a new opener $300–$450.

Opener installation →

The door opens a foot and stops

The opener's up-limit or force setting has drifted, a roller is jammed in bent track, or one spring on a two-spring door has broken while the other still lifts. Simple to diagnose in person, not worth guessing at.

Fix: from $120.

Water and cold coming in under the door

The rubber bottom seal has hardened and cracked, or the concrete has settled and left a gap. Common on Spartanburg homes built before 2005. Cheap to fix and it stops the leaves, water and mice.

Fix: bottom seal replacement, from $95.

Panels are cracked, dented or rusted through

A single dented section can sometimes be swapped — but if the manufacturer has discontinued the panel, or the bottom section has rotted, replacement is the honest answer. We will tell you which it is.

Honest Answer

Repair It, or Replace the Whole Door?

Repair — when the panels are sound

Springs, cables, rollers, hinges, sensors and openers are all wear items. A Spartanburg home's builder-grade springs are typically rated for 10,000 cycles, which is roughly seven years of normal use. Replacing them costs a fraction of a new door and buys you another decade. If the door sections are straight, square and not rusted, repair every time.

Replace — when the door itself is done

Cracked or rusted-through panels, a bottom section rotted by standing water, a door a vehicle has folded, or a discontinued panel that can no longer be matched. Also worth replacing when the repair estimate approaches half the cost of a new door — an insulated door installed starts at $1,950, and it lowers garage temperature and cuts road noise noticeably.

My garage door spring broke — can I still use the door?

No. A double-car door weighs 150–250 lbs and the springs carry all of that weight. With a broken spring the opener is holding the whole door on a plastic gear and a chain, and it can drop without warning. Do not pull the emergency release, do not try to lift it by hand, park outside and call (864) 921-7373.

How long does a garage door repair take?

Spring replacement: 45–90 minutes. Cables, rollers or sensor alignment: about an hour. Putting a door back on its track: one to two hours, depending on how much track and how many panels were damaged. Full opener installation: about two hours.

Why does my garage door close and then open right back up?

Almost always the safety sensors — the two photo-eyes near the floor on each side of the opening. A bumped bracket, a spider web, direct sunlight on the lens or a loose wire will all make the opener reverse. It can also be the down-force setting or a section binding on the track. Both are quick fixes.
