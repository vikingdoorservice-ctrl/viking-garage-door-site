Repair First, Replace Only When It Makes Sense

A lot of "dead" openers are not dead at all. Misaligned safety sensors, a stripped drive gear, a worn trolley, a failed capacitor or a remote that lost its programming are all fixable for a fraction of the cost of a new unit. We repair LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, Craftsman, Linear and most other brands. When an opener is 15–20 years old, missing parts or simply unsafe, we will tell you — and show you replacement options with prices, starting at $300 installed.

Common Opener Problems We Fix

  • Door goes down, then reverses back up (usually safety sensors)
  • Motor runs or hums but the door does not move (gear or trolley)
  • Remote or keypad stopped working
  • Opener works sometimes, or only from the wall button
  • Loud grinding, clicking or the light blinking an error code

If the opener is straining to lift the door, stop using it and check whether a spring is broken. A broken spring will burn out an opener quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my garage door reverse when it closes?

Nine times out of ten it is the safety sensors at the bottom of the tracks — misaligned, dirty, in direct sun or with a damaged wire. It is usually a quick fix.

Is it worth repairing an old opener?

If the motor is sound and parts are available, often yes. If it is over 15 years old, has no battery backup or lacks modern safety features, a new opener is usually the better value.

Which brands do you repair?

LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, Craftsman, Linear and most others. We install LiftMaster, Chamberlain and Genie.

Can you program my car or a new remote?

Yes — remotes, keypads, HomeLink car buttons and myQ smartphone control.
