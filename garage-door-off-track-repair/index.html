What Knocks a Garage Door Off Its Track

A garage door rides on rollers inside two vertical and two horizontal tracks. When a roller pops out, the door twists, binds and can hang at an angle with heavy panels and loaded springs pulling in different directions. The most common causes we see around Spartanburg and Greenville are a car or mower bumping the door, a broken cable or spring that lets one side drop, worn rollers that finally fail, and tracks that have bent or worked loose from the wall.

Is Your Door Off Track?

  • Rollers are visibly out of the track on one or both sides
  • The door is twisted, crooked or stuck partway
  • The track is bent, dented or pulled away from the wall
  • The door makes a grinding or popping noise before it stops
  • A cable is slack or hanging loose

Do not run the opener or force the door. An off-track door can fall or a cable can snap. Unplug the opener, keep people and cars clear, and call us.

How We Repair an Off-Track Door

Stabilize

We secure the door, release tension where needed and lift the panels back into alignment.

Repair track & rollers

Bent track sections are straightened or replaced, brackets re-anchored, and worn rollers swapped for quiet nylon ball-bearing rollers.

Check cables & balance

We inspect cables, drums and springs — often the real cause — then balance the door and test the opener and safety sensors.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I push the rollers back in myself?

On a very minor problem with the door fully closed, sometimes — but most off-track doors involve a loose cable or loaded spring. If the door is crooked or hanging, call a technician.

Will my door need to be replaced?

Usually not. Most off-track doors are repaired with track, roller and cable work. If a section is badly bent we can often replace just that panel.

How long does the repair take?

Most off-track repairs take one to two hours on site.

Do you come out the same day?

Yes, in most cases. A door stuck open or hanging crooked gets priority.
